# Grouped scaffold comparisons

`hop.comparison-brief/v2` introduces an explicit partition of required members
into named groups. Each group requires identical physical products at the declared
comparison stage; the groups require distinct products. These relations are
additional to global shared hairpin properties and preserved material roles.
Preparation identities remain distinct, including when products are identical.

The v1 brief retains its all-distinct or all-identical meaning and rejects the
new fields. V2 does not silently reinterpret those purposes. Existing generic
packet envelopes remain inventory containers; their embedded request version
selects strict semantics. Existing absent-field serializations remain unchanged.

The comparison design layer applies these relationships to verified complete
options, in deterministic member and option order under the existing assignment
bounds. It owns no molecular search engine. Missing members, rejected assignments
and unexamined work remain separate outcomes. Selection replays the embedded
discovery and recomputes group relationships before admitting the explicit set.

A group may reference one previously selected product through a construction
bundle, its bound selection and a molecular stage. The bundle and selection must
verify. Their exact bytes are embedded with portable relative references; no
caller-supplied molecular hash is accepted as authority. Reference stages must
match the comparison stage. Preparation permissions remain in the discovery
request; preserving a product never authorizes silent relaxation of those rules.

Exact loop words use the joined hairpin's continuous 5-prime to 3-prime strand,
from the primary arm toward its return arm. Optional finite `loop_sequences` in
foldback geometry constrain target generation; each exact target carries one
`loop_sequence`. The placement domain intersects those bases before enumeration,
and molecular replay verifies the resulting word. Omitted optional fields retain
old serializations. V2 required members can select exact loop words, separately
from permitted geometry and sequence domains. This does not predict folding,
reaction performance or biological response.

Export modules compose group rows and preparation-method columns from verified
portraits and product stages. The sheet is a projection, never a second admission
engine. A boundary sheet identifies unassigned options without claiming a common
product or inventing a completed failed molecule. Shared molecular-location
diagnostic text belongs to `export/construction/handoff`; request, discovery and
comparison presentations reuse that formatter.
